Is KKBox losing subscribers or dropping them?

Churn rose from 6.39% to 8.99% in one month while manual cancellations fell by about a third. The increase is mostly passive: members lapsing, not choosing to leave.

The question

What is driving the rise in churn between the February and March 2017 cohorts, how much of it is voluntary versus billing-related, and what does each type cost KKBox?

How it was calculated

  • Churn rate: churned members divided by total members in the cohort.
  • Voluntary churn: churned members whose last transaction had auto-renew off. Billing-related churn: churned members with auto-renew on. This is a proxy, because failed-payment records were not in the data.
  • Billing-to-voluntary ratio: billing churn rate divided by voluntary churn rate.
  • Revenue lost: the sum of the list price of the plans of churned members.
  • Payment failure rate: share of transactions with an amount paid of 0 on a priced plan, by payment method.
  • Engagement: average listening hours per member per month, with days over 24 hours removed.

What the data shows

  • Billing losses are now almost as large as voluntary ones, and growing faster.
  • Voluntary exits account for about 78% of revenue lost, while billing churners carry roughly a third of the revenue per head.
  • March billing churners were heavy listeners: about 87.5 hours, against about 106 for retained members. Many of them wanted to stay.
  • Payment methods 29 and 30 fail 8.96% and 6.04% of the time, against 1.01% for method 40 and 0.03% for method 41.

Recommendations

  • Fix payment reliability on methods 29 and 30, or steer members to more reliable methods.
  • Add payment recovery: automatic retries, reminders before card expiry, and a grace period.
  • Recover high-engagement members first.
  • Test save offers (a discount, a downgrade or a pause) for voluntary leavers on higher-priced plans.
  • A/B test recovery messages and offers before rolling them out.

Limitations

The analysis covers two cohorts only. Billing churn is inferred from the auto-renew flag, not confirmed as a failed payment. The failed-payment flag may include free trials or coupons. Revenue is list price, not the amount actually paid.
